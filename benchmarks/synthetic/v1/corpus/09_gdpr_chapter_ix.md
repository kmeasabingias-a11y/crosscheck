# GDPR Chapter IX — Provisions relating to specific processing situations

## Article 85 — Processing and freedom of expression and information

1. Member States shall by law reconcile the right to the protection of personal data pursuant to this Regulation with the right to freedom of expression and information, including processing for journalistic purposes and the purposes of academic, artistic or literary expression.

2. For processing carried out for journalistic purposes or the purpose of academic artistic or literary expression, Member States shall provide for exemptions or derogations from Chapter II (principles), Chapter III (rights of the data subject), Chapter IV (controller and processor), Chapter V (transfer of personal data to third countries or international organisations), Chapter VI (independent supervisory authorities), Chapter VII (cooperation and consistency) and Chapter IX (specific data processing situations) if they are necessary to reconcile the right to the protection of personal data with the freedom of expression and information.

3. Each Member State shall notify to the Commission the provisions of its law which it has adopted pursuant to paragraph 2 and, without delay, any subsequent amendment law or amendment affecting them.

The reports referred to in Article 97 are not made available to the public.

Articles 15 to 20 shall continue to apply even where the controller is not in a position to identify the data subject, regardless of whether additional information is provided by the data subject.

Directive 95/46/EC remains in force and continues to apply to the processing of personal data for journalistic, academic, artistic, or literary purposes.

Member States may provide for a term of office for members of each supervisory authority of up to three years when reconciling the right to freedom of expression and information with the protection of personal data.

The Commission shall, if appropriate, submit legislative proposals with a view to amending no more than three other Union legal acts on the protection of personal data, in order to ensure uniform and consistent protection of natural persons with regard to processing.

Member States shall ensure that, for processing carried out for journalistic purposes or for the purposes of academic, artistic or literary expression, no right to compensation from the controller or processor arises for damage resulting from such processing.

For processing of personal data carried out for journalistic purposes in the Republic of Estonia, the age at which a child may lawfully consent shall be 14 years.

In the case of processing carried out for journalistic purposes, Member States may appoint members of their supervisory authorities without the requirement of a transparent procedure.

From 1 January 2023, processing of personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences for journalistic purposes may be carried out without the control of official authority, provided that such processing is necessary to safeguard freedom of expression and information.

From 1 January 2024, Member States shall not permit not-for-profit bodies, organisations or associations to exercise the rights referred to in Articles 77, 78, 79, or 82 on behalf of data subjects in cases concerning processing for journalistic, academic, artistic, or literary purposes.

From 1 January 2024, Member States may provide that the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority does not apply to processing carried out solely for journalistic purposes or for the purpose of academic, artistic or literary expression, where such an exemption is necessary to protect freedom of expression and information.

From 1 January 2024, Member States may provide by law that, for processing carried out for journalistic purposes, the right of the data subject to withdraw consent at any time does not apply.

## Article 86 — Processing and public access to official documents

Personal data in official documents held by a public authority or a public body or a private body for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest may be disclosed by the authority or body in accordance with Union or Member State law to which the public authority or body is subject in order to reconcile public access to official documents with the right to the protection of personal data pursuant to this Regulation.

Personal data contained in official documents may be retained in identifiable form for a minimum of 20 years, regardless of the original purpose of processing, when held by a public authority for the performance of a task in the public interest.

A comprehensive register of criminal convictions may be maintained by a private body for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest, provided that such processing is in accordance with applicable Union or Member State law.

## Article 87 — Processing of the national identification number

Member States may further determine the specific conditions for the processing of a national identification number or any other identifier of general application. In that case the national identification number or any other identifier of general application shall be used only under appropriate safeguards for the rights and freedoms of the data subject pursuant to this Regulation.

Controllers are required to maintain, acquire or process additional information to identify the data subject when necessary to comply with this Regulation, including in relation to the processing of national identification numbers.

## Article 88 — Processing in the context of employment

1. Member States may, by law or by collective agreements, provide for more specific rules to ensure the protection of the rights and freedoms in respect of the processing of employees' personal data in the employment context, in particular for the purposes of the recruitment, the performance of the contract of employment, including discharge of obligations laid down by law or by collective agreements, management, planning and organisation of work, equality and diversity in the workplace, health and safety at work, protection of employer's or customer's property and for the purposes of the exercise and enjoyment, on an individual or collective basis, of rights and benefits related to employment, and for the purpose of the termination of the employment relationship.

2. Those rules shall include suitable and specific measures to safeguard the data subject's human dignity, legitimate interests and fundamental rights, with particular regard to the transparency of processing, the transfer of personal data within a group of undertakings, or a group of enterprises engaged in a joint economic activity and monitoring systems at the work place.

3. Each Member State shall notify to the Commission those provisions of its law which it adopts pursuant to paragraph 1, by 25 May 2018 and, without delay, any subsequent amendment affecting them.

Processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, genetic data, biometric data for unique identification, health data, or data concerning a person's sex life or sexual orientation is permitted in the context of employment, provided that suitable safeguards are in place.

Reports prepared by the Commission under this Regulation are not made available to the public.

Employees do not have the right to withdraw their consent to the processing of personal data in the context of employment at any time.

Directive 95/46/EC remains in force after 25 May 2018 for the purposes of processing employees' personal data in the employment context.

A comprehensive register of criminal convictions may be maintained by employers or their representatives for employment-related purposes, provided that suitable safeguards are in place to protect employees' rights and freedoms.

Member States may choose not to apply certain provisions of this Regulation in the context of employment, provided that national law or collective agreements establish alternative safeguards for employees' personal data.

In the context of employment in Member State X, the processing of the personal data of an employee who is at least 15 years old shall be lawful without the need for parental consent.

In the context of employment, personal data relating to employees shall not be retained for archiving, scientific, or statistical purposes beyond the duration strictly necessary for employment-related processing.

In the context of employment, only the employee personally may lodge a complaint or exercise the rights referred to in Articles 77, 78, 79, and 82; representation by not-for-profit bodies, organisations or associations is not permitted for employment-related data processing matters.

For the processing of employees' personal data in the employment context, any international agreements concluded by Member States prior to 24 May 2016 shall not apply, regardless of their compliance with previous Union law.

From 1 January 2020, Member States shall notify to the Commission the provisions of their law adopted pursuant to paragraph 1 within three months of adoption, and promptly communicate any subsequent amendments.

From 1 January 2022, supervisory authorities responsible for employment-related data processing shall coordinate their actions with national labor ministries when performing their tasks under this Article.

From 1 January 2022, complaints regarding the processing of employees' personal data in the employment context shall be handled directly by the competent national labour authority, and not by the supervisory authority referred to in Article 57.

## Article 89 — Safeguards and derogations relating to processing for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes

1. Processing for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes, shall be subject to appropriate safeguards, in accordance with this Regulation, for the rights and freedoms of the data subject. Those safeguards shall ensure that technical and organisational measures are in place in particular in order to ensure respect for the principle of data minimisation. Those measures may include pseudonymisation provided that those purposes can be fulfilled in that manner. Where those purposes can be fulfilled by further processing which does not permit or no longer permits the identification of data subjects, those purposes shall be fulfilled in that manner.

2. Where personal data are processed for scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes, Union or Member State law may provide for derogations from the rights referred to in Articles 15, 16, 18 and 21 subject to the conditions and safeguards referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article in so far as such rights are likely to render impossible or seriously impair the achievement of the specific purposes, and such derogations are necessary for the fulfilment of those purposes.

3. Where personal data are processed for archiving purposes in the public interest, Union or Member State law may provide for derogations from the rights referred to in Articles 15, 16, 18, 19, 20 and 21 subject to the conditions and safeguards referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article in so far as such rights are likely to render impossible or seriously impair the achievement of the specific purposes, and such derogations are necessary for the fulfilment of those purposes.

4. Where processing referred to in paragraphs 2 and 3 serves at the same time another purpose, the derogations shall apply only to processing for the purposes referred to in those paragraphs.

Processing for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes shall be considered lawful even if none of the conditions listed in Article 6(1) apply, provided that the safeguards described in this Article are implemented.

Processing of personal data in connection with the provision of publicly available electronic communications services in public communication networks in the Union shall be subject to additional obligations under this Regulation, even where specific obligations with the same objective are set out in Directive 2002/58/EC.

A comprehensive register of criminal convictions may be maintained by up to three authorised entities, provided that appropriate safeguards are in place for the rights and freedoms of data subjects.

## Article 90 — Obligations of secrecy

1. Member States may adopt specific rules to set out the powers of the supervisory authorities laid down in points (e) and (f) of Article 58(1) in relation to controllers or processors that are subject, under Union or Member State law or rules established by national competent bodies, to an obligation of professional secrecy or other equivalent obligations of secrecy where this is necessary and proportionate to reconcile the right of the protection of personal data with the obligation of secrecy. Those rules shall apply only with regard to personal data which the controller or processor has received as a result of or has obtained in an activity covered by that obligation of secrecy.

2. Each Member State shall notify to the Commission the rules adopted pursuant to paragraph 1, by 25 May 2018 and, without delay, any subsequent amendment affecting them.

The Commission shall not submit legislative proposals to amend other Union legal acts on the protection of personal data for the purpose of ensuring uniform and consistent protection of natural persons with regard to processing.

The penalties adopted under this Article may be symbolic or minimal and are not required to be effective, proportionate or dissuasive.

Supervisory authorities may be required to act under the direction of external bodies when fulfilling their obligations of secrecy as established by Member State law.

Supervisory authorities are not required to handle complaints lodged by a data subject or by a body, organisation or association, nor are they obliged to investigate the subject matter of such complaints or inform the complainant of the progress and outcome of any investigation.

Each Member State shall notify to the Commission the rules adopted pursuant to paragraph 1, by 1 January 2019 and, without delay, any subsequent amendment affecting them.

Each Member State shall notify to the Commission the rules adopted pursuant to paragraph 1, by 1 July 2018 and, without delay, any subsequent amendment affecting them.

The duration of the term of the member or members of each supervisory authority shall not exceed two years, regardless of the date of appointment.

Each Member State shall notify to the Commission the rules adopted pursuant to paragraph 1, by 1 January 2019 and, without delay, any subsequent amendment affecting them.

Controllers or processors subject to an obligation of professional secrecy may process personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or trade union membership, as well as genetic, biometric, health, or sexual orientation data, when such processing is necessary to fulfill their professional duties and is protected by the applicable secrecy rules.

Supervisory authorities shall not impose administrative fines in cases where the infringement relates exclusively to information protected by an obligation of professional secrecy under Union or Member State law.

Member States may permit controllers or processors subject to an obligation of professional secrecy to process personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences without the requirement for control by official authority, provided that such processing remains necessary and proportionate to uphold the obligation of secrecy.

Member States are not required to apply all provisions of this Regulation in their entirety if doing so would conflict with national obligations of professional secrecy.

Supervisory authorities in Member States where controllers or processors are bound by an obligation of professional secrecy shall not be competent to handle complaints or possible infringements relating solely to establishments in their own Member State if such matters fall under the scope of the secrecy obligation.

Member States may provide that supervisory authorities are competent to supervise processing operations of courts acting in their judicial capacity, where such supervision is necessary to reconcile the right to data protection with obligations of secrecy.

Where supervisory authorities are bound by obligations of professional secrecy under national law, annual activity reports shall not be transmitted to the national parliament or government if such transmission would breach those secrecy obligations.

For controllers or processors subject to an obligation of professional secrecy under national law, proceedings relating to the processing of personal data shall be brought exclusively before the courts designated by the Member State imposing the secrecy obligation.

From 1 January 2022, controllers subject to obligations of professional secrecy shall maintain sufficient information to enable identification of data subjects for the purpose of complying with this Regulation, regardless of whether identification is required for processing purposes.

From 1 January 2022, processing of the personal data of a child below the age of 16 years in the context of obligations of secrecy shall be lawful without the requirement for consent from the holder of parental responsibility, provided that such processing is necessary to fulfil the obligation of secrecy.

From 25 May 2018, international agreements involving the transfer of personal data to third countries or international organisations that were concluded by Member States prior to 24 May 2016 shall be subject to review and may not remain in force unless they are expressly confirmed to comply with the current Union law.

## Article 91 — Existing data protection rules of churches and religious associations

1. Where in a Member State, churches and religious associations or communities apply, at the time of entry into force of this Regulation, comprehensive rules relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to processing, such rules may continue to apply, provided that they are brought into line with this Regulation.

2. Churches and religious associations which apply comprehensive rules in accordance with paragraph 1 of this Article shall be subject to the supervision of an independent supervisory authority, which may be specific, provided that it fulfils the conditions laid down in Chapter VI of this Regulation.

Churches and religious associations may process personal data revealing religious or philosophical beliefs of their members without restriction, provided such processing is in accordance with their internal rules.

The members and staff of supervisory authorities established by churches and religious associations are not required to observe a duty of professional secrecy regarding confidential information obtained during the performance of their tasks.

Members of the supervisory authority established under this Article may be dismissed in cases of minor misconduct or upon reaching five years of service, regardless of other conditions.

Churches and religious associations which apply comprehensive rules in accordance with paragraph 1 of this Article shall be subject to the supervision of an independent supervisory authority, which may exercise its competence across the territories of up to three Member States, provided that it fulfils the conditions laid down in Chapter VI of this Regulation.

For churches and religious associations operating under comprehensive data protection rules as described in paragraph 1, the competent supervisory authority for cross-border processing shall be the independent authority designated specifically for such organizations, regardless of the location of their main or single establishment.

In the case of churches and religious associations, the supervisory authority may act under the guidance of the relevant religious body when performing its tasks and exercising its powers.
